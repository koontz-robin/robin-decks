Ben Whatley’s New MSP × Rev.ioCustomer evaluation summary
01 · Why now

A clean launch is the chance to eliminate the problems that followed the prior business.

Fragmented customer billing

Separate IT, telecom, and one-time invoices create confusion, missed payments, and an inconsistent customer experience.

Manual revenue operations

Invoice timing, ACH processing, reconciliation, distributor seats, and onsite time depend on manual attention—allowing errors to persist.

Fresh-start advantage

The new MSP can choose a simple operating model before customer volume grows, rather than migrating another patchwork later.

Evaluation thesis: The right platform should consolidate the back office, make billing dependable, and remain simple enough for a small team to operate without enterprise overhead.
Why changeDiscovery · October 19, 2026
02 · Current operating model

Strong individual tools—but important revenue and communication workflows still fall between them.

Current stack

Odoo ERPRev.io via SkySwitchSureWebPDQHuntressScreenConnectManual bank ACH

Odoo covers ticketing, CRM, and accounting. SkySwitch provides a limited Rev.io billing instance. Distributor, security, remote-support, and payment workflows remain separate.

Where work breaks down

  • Customers receive multiple invoices instead of one consolidated monthly bill.
  • Manual ACH has no automatic reconciliation.
  • Invoices can go early, on time, or late depending on intervention.
  • SureWeb seats can remain active after a customer leaves.
  • Onsite visits stopped being billed because time capture was too difficult.
  • Ticket email/SMS and internal-versus-external notes need improvement.
Current stateOdoo + SkySwitch Rev.io + manual handoffs
03 · Target future state

A simple customer-to-cash workflow designed for the new MSP.

One monthly invoice

Combine IT, telecom, hosting, consulting, marketing services, and one-time work while keeping each service clearly separated for the customer.

Flexible billing structures

Use multiple bill profiles for different schedules and parent-child accounts for districts and schools with rolled-up, itemized invoices.

Ticket-to-bill time capture

Make travel and onsite work easy to time, require useful notes, and carry approved charges into the customer’s next invoice.

Automated collections

Use autopay and automatic payment reconciliation to reduce manual bank work, missed payments, and undetected billing errors.

Connected service communication

Keep customer email/SMS, internal notes, ticket history, mobile updates, and follow-up context together.

Lean accounting model

Let Rev.io run operations and invoicing while the final accounting direction—Odoo or QuickBooks—stays intentionally simple.

Future stateOne platform · one invoice · clear ownership

05 · Illustrative ROI framework

The $1,000 monthly target can be justified by recovering a few small revenue and labor leaks.

Team efficiency

~$815 per month

Across an illustrative five users, saving just 10 minutes per workday through simpler tickets, time entry, invoicing, and communication returns about 18 hours monthly at a $45 loaded labor value.

Onsite revenue

~$650 per month

Recovering just one previously unbilled onsite hour per week produces about $650 monthly at an illustrative $150 billable rate.

Billing control

~$500 per month

Preventing or accelerating one $500 missed, late, or unreconciled charge each month creates an additional benchmark for consolidated billing and automated payments.

$1,965 monthly potential

The three scenarios total roughly $1,965 per month, or about $23,600 annually—before distributor-seat leakage, faster cash collection, or retired tools.

Fast onboarding payback

At the illustrative value level, the discussed $2,500 onboarding fee is recovered in approximately 1.3 months.

Inputs to validate

Confirm user count, labor and billable rates, onsite volume, historical billing errors, aged receivables, software costs, SureWeb leakage, and payment-processing economics.

Conservative takeaway: The investment does not require aggressive growth assumptions. Better time capture plus one avoided billing problem per month creates a practical path past the $1,000 monthly target.

Illustrative model only—not a quote or guaranteed savings. Assumptions: five users, 21.7 workdays/month, $45 loaded labor cost, $150 billable rate, one recovered onsite hour/week, and one $500 billing improvement/month.

06 · Commercial framework discussed

Flexible terms reduce commitment risk; packaging must still fit a small launch footprint.

Combined minimum

$1,000/month across telecom billing and PSA was discussed, with leadership review recommended for bundle flexibility.

Telecom billing

$500/month up to $25,000 in telecom revenue, then 3% above that threshold.

PSA users

$100/user/month was discussed, with five seats suggested as one route to the combined minimum.

Low commitment

Month-to-month terms were discussed rather than a long-term contract.

Onboarding

$2,500, split into two payments, with an average implementation period of approximately two months.

Still open

Final package, user count, Payments pricing, accounting path, integration scope, company launch date, and go-live timing remain to be confirmed.
