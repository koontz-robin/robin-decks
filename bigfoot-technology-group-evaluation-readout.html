01 · Why change now

Bigfoot wants a simpler PSA—and proof that critical service workflows will survive the move.

05 · ROI framework

The PSA price gap can be covered by roughly one recovered hour per week.

Updated case

$370–$440 monthly gap

At the discussed $1,000 monthly target, the increase over Bigfoot’s estimated $560–$630 current PSA spend is approximately $370–$440/month.

Break-even

4.6–5.5 hours per month

At an illustrative $80/hour loaded labor value, saving about 1.1–1.3 hours per week across the team covers the PSA increase.

Revenue case

1 captured hour per week

At an illustrative $150 billable rate, recovering one missed billable hour each week produces about $650/month—more than the estimated gap.

Where time comes back

Inline ticket updates, mobile time capture, agreement automation, dispatch visibility, AI-assisted notes, and fewer QuickBooks handoffs reduce administrative work.

Where leakage shrinks

Automated recurring services, ticket and project billing, accurate time capture, inventory traceability, and consolidated invoices reduce missed-charge risk.

Additional upside

The model excludes the waived $5,000 setup fee, retired BrightGauge/CPQ/time-clock costs, reduced consultant dependence, faster collections, and future voice margin.

Conservative takeaway: Bigfoot does not need a dramatic productivity gain to justify the PSA move. The remaining work is validating the assumptions with actual labor rates, billable rates, retired software costs, and measured billing leakage.

Illustrative ROI uses pricing discussed October 8 and is not a final proposal. Final economics depend on confirmed license mix, minimum, contract structure, telecom scope, and Bigfoot’s actual operating data.
