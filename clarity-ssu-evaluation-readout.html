2. Why make the change now?

The current stack is creating more friction than leverage.

Fragmentation is now the bottleneck

Clarity is using Jetbuilt, spreadsheets, Asana, project management tooling, ServiceNow, and QuickBooks Online. The problem is the lack of one connected system of record across sales, projects, service, inventory, and billing.

Growth raises the cost of handoffs

As more projects and technicians move through the business, disconnected handoffs increase the risk of missed details, slow WIP meetings, unclear ownership, and weak budget-to-actual visibility.

Past software disappointment changed the bar

Alex invested hundreds of hours into Jetbuilt before concluding key inventory and PM promises did not land. Any new platform has to prove downstream execution, not just demos.

Buying motion is active

Alex requested the demo recording and formal quote for internal review. A focused follow-up in mid-to-late August is the right moment to validate decision-critical workflows.

3. Uncovered problems

The pain is concentrated around handoffs, visibility, and adoption.

Disconnected systems

  • Duplicate data entry across sales, project, service, and finance tools
  • No unified dashboard for leadership or team synchronization
  • Manual reporting and spreadsheet dependency

Proposal-to-project risk

  • Accepted quotes need to become executable work without rework
  • Labor, product catalog, inventory, and project tasks need to stay linked
  • Jetbuilt experience created skepticism about downstream PM capability

WIP visibility

  • Biweekly WIP meetings need project dates, progress, PM ownership, schedule risk, and financial health
  • Budgeted vs. actual labor visibility is a major control point

Inventory confidence

  • Inventory is mostly project-based and moves quickly
  • Clarity needs allocation, storage, pickup, shipment, and job readiness visibility
  • Distributor catalogs must be controlled so staff quote approved products

Field adoption

  • Technicians need easy schedule, ticket, note, photo, file, time, and site history access
  • Offline mode and job-based geofencing matter for trust and usability

Implementation risk

  • Alex is cautious about spending time on another tool that does not deliver
  • Halo appeared powerful but implementation-heavy and expensive to build out

5. Requirements and systems alongside Rev.io

The next proof point is end-to-end execution, not feature name-dropping.

Current systems to replace or reduce

  • Jetbuilt for proposals
  • Excel / spreadsheets for tracking
  • Asana for tasks
  • ProjectManager.com / PM tooling
  • ServiceNow

Systems likely to remain

  • QuickBooks Online for accounting
  • Distributor catalogs where needed, with controlled product selection
  • Customer-facing and internal files that need migration or linking decisions

Required Rev.io workflows

  • CRM / opportunity to quote and proposal
  • Proposal and e-signature to project or ticket
  • Project financials, labor budget, actuals, and WIP reporting
  • Inventory reservation, fulfillment, pickup, and installed asset flow
  • Mobile technician schedule, notes, photos, files, timers, offline mode, and geofencing
Open decision areas: must-have go-live workflows, AIA vs. deposit / milestone billing, required WIP reports, launch user counts, migration scope, distributor priority, and CFO approval criteria.
