2. Why make the change now?

The current stack is making the team do the same work twice before the business scales RMR.

Nate called out the sales team’s time sink: data has to be entered in Projects360 and then again in Portal. At the same time, inventory, job costing, native mobile, onboarding, and future recurring billing are becoming higher-stakes as security and alarm services grow.

Portal handoff is too manual

Clearwater likes Portal for quoting, but the handoff into Projects360 is not the approved-proposal-to-job workflow they expected.

Field work needs a real app

Projects360’s mobile experience is described as a browser shortcut to a mobile webpage. A true native iOS/Android app is a hard requirement.

Timing needs planning

Clearwater does not want a rushed switch. They expect a multi-month decision and transition plan because prior onboarding left them to figure too much out alone.

The strategic shift is not “new PSA because new PSA.” It is putting the operational foundation in place before AV projects, service work, inventory, and security RMR pull in different directions.

3. Uncovered problems

The pain is concentrated in handoffs, field usability, and trusted job margin visibility.

Portal → Projects360 is not seamless

  • Approved proposals require too much manual movement into project execution.
  • Sales reps experience double entry across Projects360 and Portal.
  • Builder and multi-location account structures require careful billing relationship handling.

Inventory visibility is not good enough

  • Projects360 inventory management is not meeting Clearwater’s needs.
  • The demo showed stock by warehouse/truck, reservations, parts-needed queues, POs, receiving, serial tracking, transfers, deployments, and audit logs.

Mobile is a deal-breaker requirement

  • Technicians need a native app, not a webpage redirect.
  • Offline support matters at new development sites with poor cell reception.
  • Travel timers, geofencing, notes, media capture, barcode scanning, and signatures need to work in the field.

Onboarding trust is damaged

  • Projects360 onboarding left Clearwater self-teaching and building workarounds.
  • DTools is competitive partly because on-site onboarding support is attractive.
  • Rev.io must prove fast support access beyond weekly setup calls.

5. Requirements and adjacent systems

The follow-up needs to prove Rev.io fits around Portal, QuickBooks, field work, inventory, and reporting.

Systems in use today

  • Portal for quoting and proposals.
  • Projects360 for project management.
  • QuickBooks Online as accounting and customer record source of truth.
  • CompanyCam for jobsite photo storage.

Portal integration proof

  • Bidirectional sync with controlled account tagging.
  • Accepted proposals flow back with line items.
  • Duplicate detection exists, but spelling/name mismatches can still create duplicate customer records.

Project and billing workflow

  • Accepted quote creates tickets and can link to projects.
  • Project hubs cover overview, work, Gantt, and billing.
  • Deposit/milestone invoices, remaining balance, and final invoice need to be shown.

Mobile and labor capture

  • Native iOS/Android app with offline support.
  • Server-side timers, travel geofence prompts, clock in/out, job time, notes, signatures, photos, videos, and barcode scanning.
  • Payroll utilization reporting from clocked, job, and travel hours.

Inventory and job costing

  • Stock by warehouse, truck, and job.
  • Parts-needed queue and PO creation from tickets.
  • Receiving, serialized costing, reservations, deployments, transfers, audit logs, and project-level margin visibility.

Known gaps and caveats

  • No current CompanyCam integration was noted; Rev.io supports native photo/video uploads as an alternative workflow.
  • QuickBooks is not being replaced.
  • Clearwater needs clarity on support response model, not only weekly onboarding calls.
