01 · The operating vision

Build the support organization once—and scale it with every acquisition.

COEO’s combined organization needs more than another queue. It needs a common service model: consistent intake, intelligent assignment, measurable commitments, complete time capture and decision-ready reporting across one support team.

1 · Detect

Bring system alerts and human requests into a governed intake process.

2 · Correlate

Group related events and establish the affected customer, site and service context.

3 · Route

Direct work by urgency, skill, product and customer commitment.

4 · Resolve

Give technicians context, workflows, communication tools and time tracking.

5 · Learn

Turn every interaction into SLA, profitability and client-review insight.

The organizational prize

A unified support team working from one consolidated ticketing platform, with shared processes and clear ownership instead of inherited operating silos.

The capacity prize

Automation absorbs repetitive intake, routing, updates and administration so staff can spend more time diagnosing, resolving and improving service.

Sourced to 7, 13, 16 and 19 Oct. discussions. The five-stage operating model is a synthesis of the buyer’s stated workflow.

Routing lacks a common playbook

Tickets can move across Tier 1 shifts before escalation. The combined team needs assignment that reflects product, skill, urgency and customer context.

SLA truth is hard to see

Product- and customer-level commitments are still being documented. The team suspects misses but lacks dependable, shared compliance metrics.

Economics are obscured

Support effort by customer and product, complete status history, dispatch frequency and site availability are difficult to assemble for leadership and clients.

04 · The impact thesis

Ticketing ROI comes from capacity, service control and agreement economics.

01

Release capacity

Reduce repetitive intake, duplicate handling, manual routing and one-by-one outage communication. Reinvest hours in resolution, prevention and customer work.

02

Protect service outcomes

Prioritize against documented commitments, reduce avoidable rework and make accountability visible before issues become escalations.

03

See agreement profitability

Capture technician hours consistently, compare consumed effort with agreement economics and expose where pricing, scope or process must change.

04

Upgrade client reviews

Use credible SLA, availability, incident and support-demand evidence to guide business reviews toward outcomes and improvement plans.

05

Scale the operating model

Onboard teams and future acquisitions into repeatable queues, workflows, ownership rules and reporting instead of adding administrative load linearly.

06

Strengthen management

Give leaders a shared fact base for staffing, coaching, customer decisions, process redesign and automation priorities.

Impact thesis derived only from ticketing outcomes in the reviewed meetings. No financial benefit is claimed without an owned baseline.

06 · Joint workflow & design validation

Turn the shared vision into observable proof.

The meetings established a collaborative design path: use COEO’s real service scenarios to shape workflows, integrations and acceptance evidence without presuming the finished configuration.

1

Baseline

Measure two weeks of intake touches, handling time, incident updates, routing transfers, SLA outcomes and time-capture completeness.

2

Design

Agree the support taxonomy, assignment ownership, SLA rules, linked-ticket behavior, customer communications and source data.

3

Prove

Run production-like SolarWinds bursts, multi-site incidents, escalations, tracked time and client-review reporting with named evaluators.

4

Accept

Document results, owners, service thresholds, rollout sequence, adoption measures and the ticketing investment used in the ROI case.

Proof scenarios to co-design

  • Alert grouping, customer/site context and recovery handling
  • Skills-, product-, priority- and SLA-aware assignment
  • One-to-many outage updates with controlled customer communication
  • Response/resolution timers, pauses, reopen behavior and escalation
  • Technician time capture tied to agreement consumption

Decision evidence to produce

  • Measured hours released and an explicit redeployment plan
  • Reliable SLA and service-accountability dashboard
  • Agreement effort and profitability view by customer/product
  • Client business review with availability and exception trends
  • Phased operating plan that the combined support team owns
16 Oct. used a mock SolarWinds dataset to illustrate the intended triage workflow; 19 Oct. expanded the joint design discussion. Production acceptance criteria and dates were not finalized in the reviewed record.
Executive decision frame
Advance New Rev.io through a ticketing proof phase built around COEO’s real workflows—and judge it by measurable capacity release, SLA execution, tracked-hours profitability, client-review quality and operational adoption.

The destination is clear: one support team, one consolidated ticketing platform and a scalable service model. The next step is to convert that vision into jointly accepted evidence and an ROI case grounded in COEO’s own baseline.
