## Business Issue Details
Crown Networks is re-evaluating Rev.io after a prior evaluation stalled because project management was not mature enough at the time. Crown currently uses ConnectWise, but Ron described it as clunky, outdated, time-consuming, and difficult to fully configure. The business needs a better way to manage project work, quoting, work orders, subcontractor coordination, invoicing, payments, vendor costs, and reporting without repeating another painful PSA implementation.
## Problems with Current Systems
- ConnectWise took nearly two years to get functioning the way Crown needs it.
- Project managers only use ConnectWise minimally because project workflows are too clunky.
- Detailed project work orders are built in Word, saved in OneDrive/file servers, converted to PDFs, and sent to subcontractors.
- Project data is scattered across Word, Excel, OneDrive, file folders, ConnectWise, and accounting tools.
- Crown uses up to ~160 subcontractors, making full-user licensing and mobile workflows challenging.
- WiseSync/WisePay were recently adopted, but QuickBooks sync still creates errors that accounting has to fix.
- Crown has not fully recouped the time and cost invested in ConnectWise onboarding.
- Ron is open to switching but cautious about repeating a long, expensive implementation.
## Rev.io Solutions They Would Use
- Project management: Project phases, tickets, milestones, project activity, task tracking, Gantt/table views, and centralized project execution.
- Progressive billing: Deposits, project billing, milestone/progress invoicing, and billing tied to project work.
- Quoting: Quote templates, product catalog, built-in e-signature, and quote-to-ticket/project workflow.
- Work order support: Use project/ticket details, templates, documents, and Revy AI to help streamline detailed subcontractor work order creation.
- Ticketing / service desk: Ticket workflows, time tracking, assignments, status management, and customer/project history.
- Vendor and distributor integrations: Ingram Micro, TD Synnex, ADI, Pax8, pricing/cost updates, and margin visibility.
- Payments and QuickBooks Online: Native payment processing, surcharging, payment portal, invoice/payment flow, and QBO sync to reduce WiseSync/WisePay complexity.
- Reporting and analytics: Project profitability, margins, work status, invoice/payment visibility, and operational dashboards.
- Revy AI: Create tickets/customers/reports, assist with project/work-order summaries, and reduce manual admin work.
- Mobile app: Useful for internal users, but should be positioned carefully because Crown relies heavily on subcontractors.
## Value
Rev.io can create value for Crown by giving them a more usable project and quote-to-project workflow than ConnectWise, while reducing scattered documentation and manual accounting reconciliation. The biggest value is not just replacing ConnectWise — it is helping Ron’s small team run projects, work orders, subcontractors, vendor costs, invoicing, payments, and reporting with less friction. If Rev.io can prove project management is easier and implementation is smoother, Crown could avoid another renewal cycle with a platform they already view as clunky.
## Power
Ron is the CTO and appears to be the primary decision maker. He said he can sign if Rev.io becomes the solution of choice. Andy owns the sales follow-up, and Cam helped position Rev.io’s onboarding, partnership model, and platform improvements. A future demo should likely include Ron and any project/accounting stakeholders who own project workflow, work orders, subcontractor coordination, and QuickBooks/payment processes.

## Risk and Mitigation Plan
Risk: Ron is hesitant to switch because ConnectWise implementation was long, expensive, and painful.  
Mitigation: Lead with Rev.io’s onboarding process, structured training, migration support, and month-to-month model. Show that Rev.io is easier to configure and adopt than ConnectWise.
Risk: Crown recently invested in WiseSync/WisePay, creating sunk-cost hesitation.  
Mitigation: Position Rev.io’s native payment processing and QBO sync as a way to reduce ongoing sync errors and simplify the payment/accounting stack over time.
Risk: Subcontractor workflow may not fit a normal full-user licensing model.  
Mitigation: Focus the demo on internal project manager workflows, PDF/work order generation, project/ticket documentation, and external subcontractor coordination without assuming all subcontractors need licenses.
Risk: Project management was the reason the prior Rev.io evaluation stalled.  
Mitigation: Make project management the centerpiece of the demo and show phases, milestones, tickets, project billing, and progressive billing in detail.
Risk: Crown is a small team, so switching cost and disruption matter.  
Mitigation: Keep the business case focused on time savings, fewer sync errors, simpler project execution, and avoiding another year in a system they consider clunky.
Risk: Mobile app may not be a major differentiator because most work is subcontracted.  
Mitigation: Mention mobile as a value-add, but do not over-index on it. Prioritize project/work-order/subcontractor and accounting workflows.
## Key Next Step
The next step is to schedule a focused demo for Ron that proves Rev.io’s current project management, progressive billing, work order, subcontractor coordination, and QBO/payment workflows are materially better than Crown’s current ConnectWise process.
