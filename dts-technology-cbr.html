Account story

DTS brings real support to technology services that are rarely simple behind the scenes.

DTS supports customers across devices, business internet, wireless plans, managed router services, VoiceLinQ communications, IT support, and related service add-ons. That model creates a healthy mix of recurring services, one-time charges, support requests, and customer changes — exactly where a clean billing and payments foundation matters.

Customer-first support

DTS presents a support-led brand promise: real people, responsive help, and clear paths for IT, wireless, VoiceLinQ, and device support.

Multi-product technology model

Public DTS offerings include connectivity, routers, device ordering, VoiceLinQ UC/contact center, installation, equipment protection, and managed services.

Operational complexity

As offerings grow, the customer experience depends on accurate invoicing, clear payment options, clean handoffs, and fast issue resolution.

Review themes

What we should confirm together.

The best CBR is not a product tour. It is a shared checkup on business outcomes, pain points, and next priorities.

Billing accuracy and customer experience

  • Are products, add-ons, one-time fees, and recurring services showing up accurately?
  • Where does DTS still need manual review before invoices go out?
  • Are there invoice formats, reporting views, or customer-facing details that would reduce questions?

Payments and cash collection

  • How are customers paying today, and where can autopay/card-on-file adoption improve?
  • Are failed payments and past-due follow-ups easy to manage?
  • Does payment reconciliation match the finance team’s ideal workflow?

Bill-on-Behalf workflow

  • Which customers or use cases are live today?
  • What is working well in quoting, billing, margin visibility, and support?
  • Where can Rev.io help remove friction or ambiguity?

Growth and partner motion

  • How is DTS thinking about growth across devices, wireless, VoiceLinQ, IT support, and managed services?
  • Are there partner/channel workflows Rev.io should understand?
  • What should be prioritized over the next quarter?
Platform fit

Where Rev.io can keep supporting DTS.

The goal is to connect platform capabilities to DTS outcomes: fewer manual touches, cleaner collections, better visibility, and a smoother customer experience.

Automate routine billing work

Reduce recurring manual effort around invoice prep, adjustments, service changes, and customer billing questions.

Improve payment visibility

Review payment adoption, failed-payment handling, AR reporting, and reconciliation so finance has fewer loose threads.

Support product expansion

Make sure new offers — like UC seats, router services, support packages, and installation charges — can be billed consistently as DTS expands.

CBR working assumption: if DTS can remove even 3–5 manual billing or reconciliation hours per month, that returns 36–60 hours per year to higher-value customer and finance work. We should validate the actual number together with invoice volume, AR aging, payment method mix, and manual adjustment counts.
Roadmap discussion

Recommended topics for the next stage of the partnership.

These are customer-facing discussion areas, not assumptions. The CBR should confirm which ones matter most to DTS.

Billing workflow optimization

Validate invoice structure, service catalog setup, add-on billing, BoB handling, and exception processes.

Payments adoption plan

Identify where DTS can increase electronic payment usage, reduce friction, and improve collection visibility.

Reporting and executive visibility

Confirm the reports DTS leadership needs for revenue, customer activity, billing exceptions, and AR.

Action plan

Leave the CBR with owners, dates, and decisions.

A useful follow-up plan should be specific enough for both teams to execute without needing to decode meeting notes later.
