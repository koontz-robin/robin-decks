02 · Forward Edge strategic initiatives

The platform decision should advance efficiency, customer experience, acquisition readiness, and AI adoption.

Resolve work correctly the first time

The primary operating goal is greater efficiency: solve issues on the first interaction, eliminate callbacks, and avoid unnecessary escalations to higher-level staff.

Platform implication: technicians need trusted customer history, clear workflows, usable knowledge, and complete operational context at the point of service.

Strategic priority

Raise the customer-experience standard

Forward Edge wants faster response and a better service experience—even though customers are not currently complaining. The objective is proactive improvement, not reactive repair.

Platform implication: preserve customer reporting access while improving responsiveness, visibility, and consistency.

Strategic priority

Build an acquisition-ready operating model

Forward Edge is in a growth phase centered on acquisitions and needs tools to analyze an acquired company’s systems, consolidate data, and standardize operations faster.

Platform implication: repeatable migration, system analysis, data normalization, and multi-entity visibility become core growth capabilities.

Growth priority

Scale AI capability across the workforce

AI is a major focus. Forward Edge aims to have 60% of employees Gemini AI Professional certified by year end, eventually reaching everyone, with training across finance, sales, and other teams.

Rev.io shared that AI has made data migration and system consolidation substantially faster and easier.

Platform implication: evaluate where AI can accelerate migration, consolidation, analysis, and employee workflows without compromising data trust.

04 · Visibility and performance gaps

Critical management views require manual work—and some service data is not trusted enough to share.

Reporting visibility

Useful reports can require roughly 8 hours per month of manual work. Purchasing history is difficult to filter across vendor-name variations, while leadership wants visibility by customer, business segment, and solution set.

Forward Edge uses Power BI to give customers access to reports for their own environments because ConnectWise did not provide the desired customer data sharing.

Potential impact: a separate reporting layer adds cost and administration; replacing it without preserving customer access could diminish the service experience.

Sourced

Agreement profitability

Profitability is hard to see consistently across roughly 200 agreements. ConnectWise reporting can depend on how an agreement was invoiced, forcing third-party tools and manual exports to identify low-margin contracts.

Must-have: consistent profitability visibility.

Potential impact: margin leakage and slower correction of underperforming contracts.

Sourced

Lack of trust in service metrics

Service reporting relies on Cognition, custom reports, and API work, with data pulled nightly rather than in real time. The service leader can obtain some technician performance data but does not trust it enough to publish.

Potential impact: weaker coaching and accountability, less confidence in EOS scorecards, and slower identification of service issues.

Sourced
05 · Workflow and experience gaps

Calendar constraints, fragmented interfaces, and email-based field work create measurable drag.

Project management calendars

The current platform assumes five eight-hour days, while field crews work four 10-hour days. Projected end dates and calendars can therefore be inaccurate.
