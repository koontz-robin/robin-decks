01 · What Forward Edge said

The change is about control, usable data, and growth, not feature volume.

“We’re a software sprawl and we’re getting too big… It’s not sustainable.”

Reporting visibility takes too much work

Useful reports can require roughly eight hours of manual work. Vendor-name variations make purchasing history difficult to filter, while leadership needs views by customer, business segment, and solution set.

Core work crosses too many systems

Salesforce, QuoteWerks, ConnectWise, Monday.com, DeskDirector and Cognition 360 divide one operating process across separate tools.

VAR and onsite work are non-negotiable

Product-only sales orders, purchasing, receiving, rack/bin locations, serialization, project billing, mobile field work and school-specific scheduling all matter.

The migration must be safe

Forward Edge has roughly 125 internal users, a long ConnectWise history, ~200 agreements, and workflows that cannot be lost in transition.

02 · Forward Edge strategic initiatives

The platform decision should advance efficiency, customer experience, acquisition readiness, and AI adoption.

Resolve work correctly the first time

The primary operating goal is greater efficiency: solve issues on the first interaction, eliminate callbacks, and avoid unnecessary escalations to higher-level staff.

Platform implication: technicians need trusted customer history, clear workflows, usable knowledge, and complete operational context at the point of service.

Raise the customer-experience standard

Forward Edge wants faster response and a better service experience, even though customers are not currently complaining. The objective is proactive improvement, not reactive repair.

Platform implication: preserve customer reporting access while improving responsiveness, visibility, and consistency.

Build an acquisition-ready operating model

Forward Edge is in a growth phase centered on acquisitions and needs tools to analyze an acquired company’s systems, consolidate data, and standardize operations faster.

Platform implication: repeatable migration, system analysis, data normalization, and multi-entity visibility become core growth capabilities.

Scale AI capability across the workforce

AI is a major focus. Forward Edge aims to have 60% of employees Gemini AI Professional certified by year end, eventually reaching everyone, with training across finance, sales, and other teams.

Rev.io shared that AI has made data migration and system consolidation substantially faster and easier.

Platform implication: evaluate where AI can accelerate migration, consolidation, analysis, and employee workflows without compromising data trust.

03 · Business consequences and urgency

The status quo consumes capacity, delays insight, and leaves money tied up in manual operations.

Employee time

Eight-plus hours can be spent assembling a useful financial view. A purchasing agent also spends 1–5 hours each week manually updating PO tracking.

Cash flow and revenue timing

Projects, reservations and billing are separated today. Forward Edge needs clearer visibility into uninvoiced reserved items and flexible progress billing.

Capacity and rework

Scheduling in Monday.com is largely manual; resource conflicts recur; employees navigate duplicate systems and handoffs.

Operating cost

Invoice-format changes were cited at about $2,500 and 3+ months; paid professional services were cited at about $250/hour. The confirmed core stack—ConnectWise, Monday.com, QuoteWerks, and DeskDirector—costs approximately $164,000–$165,000 annually.

Customer and employee experience

Forward Edge bought DeskDirector because the ConnectWise experience was not acceptable and currently lacks a mobile app for technicians.

Decision runway

A Q4 2026 choice preserves implementation runway before the July 2027 contract end. Rev.io estimated 4–5 months for setup, migration, testing and training.

04 · Visibility and performance gaps

Critical management views require manual work, and some service data is not trusted enough to share.

Reporting visibility

Useful reports can require roughly 8 hours per month of manual work. Purchasing history is difficult to filter across vendor-name variations, while leadership wants visibility by customer, business segment, and solution set.

Forward Edge uses Power BI to give customers access to reports for their own environments because ConnectWise did not provide the desired customer data sharing.

Potential impact: a separate reporting layer adds cost and administration; replacing it without preserving customer access could diminish the service experience.

Agreement profitability

Profitability is hard to see consistently across roughly 200 agreements. ConnectWise reporting can depend on how an agreement was invoiced, forcing third-party tools and manual exports to identify low-margin contracts. The model assumes 30 minutes/week of review effort, 75% eliminated, plus $50/month gross-profit recovery across 10 addressable agreements.

Must-have: consistent profitability visibility.

Potential impact: margin leakage and slower correction of underperforming contracts.

Lack of trust in service metrics

Service reporting relies on Cognition, custom reports, and API work, with data pulled nightly rather than in real time. The service leader can obtain some technician performance data but does not trust it enough to publish.

Potential impact: weaker coaching and accountability, less confidence in EOS scorecards, and slower identification of service issues.

05 · Workflow and experience gaps

Calendar constraints, fragmented interfaces, and email-based field work create measurable drag.

Project management calendars

The current platform assumes five eight-hour days, while field crews work four 10-hour days. Projected end dates and calendars can therefore be inaccurate.

Value: approximately 146 hours/year recovered, or $6,570/year at $45/hour.

Assumes 10 technicians × 15 minutes/week plus one scheduling hour/week, with 80% eliminated. Schedule corrections only; mobile savings are separate.

Field technicians lack a mobile app

Technicians are not using the ConnectWise mobile experience and rely heavily on email.

Value: 40 hours/month or 480 hours/year redirected toward service work; approximately $21,600/year.

10 techs × 3 visits/day × 20 days/month × 5 minutes × 80% adoption. Dispatcher savings excluded.

Outdated UI/UX creates another interface

The portal and technician experience were described as antiquated, slow to refresh, and burdened with too many fields to train users on. Forward Edge added DeskDirector as a front end.

Value: approximately $5,000–$6,000/year in addressable software spend if customer access and experience are preserved.

Potential impact: slower staff and customer workflows, added administration, and avoidable software cost.

09 · How Rev.io supports the operating model

A connected foundation for service, projects, inventory, billing, analytics, and field operations.

Agreements, projects, and billing

  • Recurring, block-hour, and time-and-material agreement structures
  • Project templates, phases, Gantt planning, and time/budget rollups
  • Flexible billing across recurring service, project milestones, and product work
  • Agreement and project profitability visibility

Inventory and purchasing

  • Inventory locations, serialization, reservations, and stock views
  • Purchase orders linked to operational workflows
  • Mobile scanning and parts reservation
  • Visibility across warehouse and field activity

Service and field operations

  • Ticketing, dispatch, and calendar integration
  • Mobile calendar, tickets, geofencing, and offline cache
  • Quoting and e-signature
  • Shared operational data across teams

Consolidation, analytics, and migration

  • Knowledgebase and password vault replacing IT Glue
  • Graphic dashboards, SLA/utilization, and customer-facing reporting replacing Cognition360 and Power BI
  • Salesforce opportunities sync to Rev.io for quoting; QuickBooks remains the accounting system
  • API migration for core customer, service, financial, and asset records
