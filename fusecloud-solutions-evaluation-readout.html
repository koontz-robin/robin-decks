2 · What Fuse told us

“Our PSA should be a force multiplier.” Today, project status is rebuilt in a weekly spreadsheet, billing and ticket context is hard to follow, and growth requires automations and integrations—not more handoffs.

Project control is duplicated

A sales coordinator and project team member update project standing weekly. Walter’s spreadsheet repeats Classic Rev.io data because the system does not provide the needed shared view.

Profitability is opaque

Fuse cannot see labor plus product cost by customer today. That blocks confident pricing, account intervention, and the EBITDA visibility needed while managed IT expands.

Revenue leakage is real

Fuse cited paying for 10 Microsoft licenses while billing for 9. Similar supplier-to-agreement variances can silently erode recurring margin.

Service pods need routing

Multiple pods are expected in two to three years. Incoming work must reach the right pod, individual assignment must scale, and managers need targeted notifications.

SLA evidence matters

Larger customers increasingly ask for a documented SLA process. Fuse needs real-time status and escalation evidence, not a policy that the system cannot reliably enforce.

The future state

Quote once; create the right order, project, ticket, inventory and billing actions; serve from one customer context; reconcile what Fuse buys versus bills; measure margin and service health continuously.

6 · Acceptance plan

Turn the decision into six evidence gates.

01

Baseline the value

Time the weekly spreadsheet and quote-to-project setup. Export ticket volume/touches, license variances, tool costs, loaded labor, SLA credits and profitability gaps. Name Finance as calculation approver.

02

Run the December workflow

After Viking 3, use one real new-service order and one MAC: signed quote → order → project/template → tickets/assets → recurring billing → milestone/change → portal status. Save evidence.

03

Repair and load-test service

Prove SLA pause/resume on customer reply, escalation, group and individual routing, pod notifications, outage linking, mobile/offline work, attachment behavior and reporting.

04

Validate the ecosystem

Document direction, data objects, cadence, ownership and failure handling for HubSpot, Hudu, NinjaOne, Pax8, Quoter/ScalePad, PRTG, Backup Radar, CSAT and incident response.

05

Paper migration + commercials

Define records/history moved, reconciliation tolerances, coexistence, cutover, rollback and support. Confirm 50-user pricing, included seats, $750 hold item, implementation, integrations and payment triggers.

06

Make a gated decision

Score all 27 Must Haves as accepted, failed, workaround or future. Link proof, owner and date. Contract critical commitments and do not book dependent benefit until its gate passes.

7 · Highest-value questions for Bryan

Close these gaps before presenting a “quantified” business case.

1. What is the cost of today’s manual work?

How many people and hours per week maintain the project spreadsheet, rekey signed quotes, create projects/orders, reconcile billing and chase status? What is each role’s fully loaded cost?

ROI critical

2. How large is verified leakage?

Across Microsoft, Pax8 and other suppliers, what is the 12-month value of units paid versus units billed, by category? What portion is recoverable revenue and what is its true contribution margin?

ROI critical

3. What service capacity can be released?

Monthly ticket volume by pod/work type? Median touches and admin minutes? Current endpoint-to-tech ratio, utilization and hiring plan? Which time savings become capacity versus cash savings?

Avoid overclaiming

4. What is the cost of service failure?

How many SLA misses, credits, escalations and at-risk renewals occurred in the last year? Which were caused by routing, visibility or the reply/resume behavior? What retention value is legitimately attributable?

Impact + proof

5. What can actually be retired?

Which contracts, modules and internal spreadsheets disappear, at what renewal date, and who approves retirement? Which systems—especially HubSpot and specialist monitoring—remain by design?

No double count

6. What is the decision function?

Who owns the decision, who signs economics, which 27 Must Haves are hard gates, how does Halo score, what is the decision date, and what happens if December 1 or the migration criteria are missed?

Qualification

7. What is the all-in investment?

Confirm named-user count, included seats, implementation, migration, training, integration work, internal time, ongoing support, AI usage, retained-tool cost, contract term and price protection.

ROI denominator

8. What does acceptance look like?

For every critical workflow, name test data, pass/fail outcome, evidence, business owner and due date. Which commitments must be contractual, and who can accept a workaround?

Decision control
