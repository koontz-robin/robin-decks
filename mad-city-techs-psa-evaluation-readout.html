1. Who is Mad City Techs?

A Madison-area technology support provider built around practical, local service.

Business profile

Mad City Techs positions as a computer and technology specialist offering business IT consulting, repair, configuration, virus detection, and practical technology support at competitive prices.

Service model

Customer reviews emphasize responsive local help, reasonable pricing, and support both over the phone and in person. Kevin and Dave confirmed technicians go onsite frequently.

Why PSA fit matters

The PSA must support a hands-on MSP workflow: ticket intake, scheduling, onsite work, time capture, invoicing, agreements, configurations, documentation, and customer-specific knowledge.

Mad City Techs
2. Why make the change now?

ConnectWise has become more expensive to work around than to trust.

Modular friction is blocking value

Project management is technically present, but practical usage runs into additional modules. Project work is not visible on the normal service board, reinforcing the feeling of disconnected compartments.

Billing trust is damaged

ConnectWise applied billable time against expired block-hour agreements. The first discovered issue was roughly $22,000, and the bug may have existed for at least 3.5 years with no clear fix.

Mobile is not field-ready

The mobile app is clunky for notes and time entry, cannot generate invoices, makes ticket closing painful, and the web experience on mobile is effectively non-functional.

Partnership has worn thin

Account managers change frequently, success management feels more like upsell than problem solving, and after 15 years Mad City Techs is inviting alternatives into the conversation.

3. Uncovered problems

The next demo has to prove everyday work, not just advanced feature coverage.

Expired agreement leakage

Billable time has been applied to expired block-hour agreements, creating revenue leakage and forcing Mad City Techs to pay ConnectWise for reporting help just to identify the missing time.

Day-to-day service flow must be clean

Kevin and Dave specifically asked to see how tickets come in, how they are captured, how they are scheduled, how time entry works, and how that flows into invoicing.

Current quoting is manual

Quotes are handled in spreadsheets, contracts are signed in person or via PDF/scan, and there is no e-signature platform today — creating a clear opportunity for native quoting and e-signature.

Configurations and KB matter

Mad City Techs uses customer configurations and KB articles today. Rev.io needs to show customer-specific documentation, repeatable procedures, and how configurations tie back to service work.

4K invoice rendering risk

Invoice text becomes unreadably small on 4K monitors in ConnectWise. Patrick needs to confirm Rev.io’s desktop experience and invoice workflow render correctly on 4K displays.

QuickBooks Desktop clarity

They moved from QuickBooks Online to Desktop because ConnectWise did not support the prior path. Rev.io needs to confirm Desktop integration scope, including API versus batch export.
