2. Decision context

The switching case is real, but Halo has reduced the cost and timing risk.

Autotask/Kaseya exit

NCI wants to separate from its current ecosystem before the July 2027 expiration and retain a contingency path if service continuity becomes unfriendly.

Halo’s current position

Halo is priced slightly lower, offers free platform access until Autotask expires, claims a 2–3 month API migration, and already integrates with Neo. NCI’s engineers received a Halo refresh demo in mid-September.

Rev.io’s counter-position

Month-to-month terms, native billing, flexible agreements, SureTax, built-in dashboards, integrated payments, and a single connected service-to-cash workflow create differentiated operational value.

Decision path

Technicians and the help-desk team validate usability and integrations. Samantha and finance validate billing and economics. Troy and Samantha then bring a recommendation to leadership.

The decision is no longer “can Rev.io function as a PSA?” It is “can Rev.io preserve the two workflows NCI cannot afford to lose?”

3. Technical workflow proof

The September 16 demo confirmed broad day-to-day service-desk coverage.

Ticketing + SLA

Boards and personalized views map to NCI’s 4–6 queues. Inline edits, group assignment, configurable SLA indicators, templates, bidirectional email, outbound SMS, attachments, and AI-assisted note cleanup were demonstrated.

Dispatch + time

Drag-and-drop scheduling, utilization thresholds, calendar sync, persistent and concurrent timers, separate payroll time, reason-code billing logic, and conversational time entry were shown.

Mobile field work

iOS/Android support includes queues, schedules, summaries, GPS/geofencing, offline caching, files and photos, time capture, customer history, dashboards, and parts/asset scanning.

Projects

Templates, phases, dependencies, milestones, Gantt views, budgeted time, centralized project billing, deposits, partial billing, payment schedules, and job-cost visibility were confirmed. Kirk’s deeper workflow questions remain open.

Analytics

Read-only SQL access and Revy-generated reporting can turn natural-language requests into saved queries, charts, and dashboards without a separate analytics add-on.

Migration

Rev.io prefers an Autotask API migration and has prior experience. NCI wants its data-engineering team involved; exact coverage, validation, and fallback requirements still need a documented plan.

4. Billing and sales proof

The September 21 session strengthened Rev.io’s clearest advantage.

Quotes + proposals

One-time and recurring items can share a proposal. Catalog search, bundles/macros, bulk pricing adjustments, branded templates, tokens, multiple signers, e-signature, expiration controls, and version history were demonstrated.

Agreements

Recurring charges, multiple block-hour pools, time and materials, automated coverage rules, agreement-level profitability, and margin trends support NCI’s varied client arrangements.

Billing control

One-time work can invoice directly or enter a consolidated billing queue. Recurring billing can automate calculation, invoice creation, delivery, and payment as independently controlled gates.

Tax simplification

SureTax calculates from service location and product setup across jurisdictions, replacing tax districts and codes that NCI currently maintains manually in Autotask and QuickBooks.

Confirmed workflow gap

Rich opportunity context and source/purchase links can live in a proposal, but they do not automatically flow into downstream tickets or recurring services. NCI relies on that handoff for purchasing accuracy.

Automation boundary

Recurring charges can be fully automated. Variable and one-time charges still require review and approval—consistent with NCI’s need for financial control.

5. Integration decision gate

Two established workflows are hard blockers—not wishlist items.

Neo AI dispatcher · hard blocker

NCI invested roughly ten months configuring Neo for triage and automatic dispatch. Rev.io has no native connector today, and NCI has no dedicated manual dispatcher to bridge a gap.

Native roadmap: AI routing by skills, sentiment, technician cost, and workload was discussed for January–February 2027. API-based custom integration may be possible but is not yet committed.

MSP Process · hard blocker

NCI has used MSP Process for years, including two-factor identity verification during client calls. Feasibility and timing were escalated to Rev.io’s integrations team after the September 21 demo.

Quote-context handoff

Rev.io needs a target date or workaround for carrying internal opportunity context and source links into fulfillment tickets and recurring services.

Other ecosystem validation

Close the loop on Hudu scope, Ninja asset and billing behavior, QBO posting, Ingram/Pax8 reconciliation, barcode workflow, and field-user laptop access.

Win condition: provide written feasibility, ownership, target dates, interim workflows, and commercial treatment for both blockers—not simply “on the roadmap.”

6. Commercial picture

Rev.io’s baseline is clear; the comparison still needs a total-cost view.

Standard users

28 × $100/month = $2,800/month, including the full platform and mobile app. Fees begin at signature under month-to-month, 30-day terms.

Field users

$30/user/month for mobile schedule and ticket access. NCI’s field staff also use laptops, so license eligibility and practical workflow need clarification.

Onboarding

$2,500 one time: 50% at signature and 50% at graduation, estimated near three months. Includes a dedicated consultant, recurring sessions, walkthroughs, and video training.

Revy AI

15,000 credits/month included; overage is $0.01 per credit. Built-in summaries, report generation, and note revision were described as non-credit-consuming functions.

Payments opportunity

NCI reported approximately $300K/month of processing. Compare Rev.io Payments with ConnectBooster using merchant statements, including effective rates, reconciliation, surcharge handling, and cash-flow impact.

Competitive reality

NCI currently sees Rev.io as slightly more expensive and less integrated than Halo. The business case must quantify billing labor, tax maintenance, reporting, contract flexibility, and retained-tool savings.

7. Risk and mitigation plan

Turn every open concern into a dated proof point.

Integration loss

Risk: Neo or MSP Process cannot be preserved.
Mitigation: written technical assessment, API path, committed roadmap dates, interim design, named owners, and acceptance criteria.

Migration incompleteness

Risk: Autotask contracts, history, or configuration fail to transfer.
Mitigation: object-level scope, sample extraction, reconciliation tests, cutover plan, rollback controls, and NCI engineering participation.

Operational handoff gap

Risk: approved quote context is lost before procurement.
Mitigation: demo a workaround now and provide an enhancement target for automated downstream context.

Timeline compression

Risk: a year-end decision leaves insufficient runway.
Mitigation: define standard and accelerated implementation plans with minimum viable go-live modules.

Pricing disadvantage

Risk: Halo wins on near-term cash and license cost.
Mitigation: model transition overlap, retained tools, billing effort, tax administration, payments economics, and negotiated referral pricing.

Vendor stability

Risk: acquisition anxiety remains unresolved.
Mitigation: separately address requested no-sell/change-of-control language; no commitment has been made.
