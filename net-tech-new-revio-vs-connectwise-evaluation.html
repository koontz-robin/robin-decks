3 · Decision-based motives

The evaluation hinges on project control, operational visibility, and confidence that no critical ConnectWise capability gets lost.

Project schedules do not cascade

When a client delay or resource change moves one task, downstream work must be rescheduled manually. That burden discourages teams from planning far enough ahead.

Skills + multi-person scheduling

Complex projects require matching technicians by skill, scheduling several people to the same work, and moving the whole block together across shared calendars.

Inventory lives outside the PSA

ConnectWise inventory was never adopted. SharePoint lists and custom orchestration now manage hardware and rental assets, leaving a fragmented audit trail.

Project profitability arrives late

Net Tech needs real-time material and labor cost visibility—not a post-project exercise to learn whether the work made money.

Mobile adoption is weak

Only three ConnectWise mobile licenses rotate among users and see limited use. Technicians need one practical app for schedule, notes, time, parts, signatures, and history.

Migration risk is real

Nineteen years of data and workflows create understandable caution. A feature map, roadmap transparency, proven migration tooling, and comparable references are essential.

7 · Where the platforms are similar

Both platforms cover the PSA foundation Net Tech needs to operate an MSP.

Service management

Both support centralized tickets, service boards, priorities, technician assignment, time entry, SLAs, customer communication, and dispatch workflows.

Projects + profitability

Both organize project work, labor, materials, budgets, milestones, billing, and reporting so delivery teams can connect execution to financial outcomes.

Agreements + recurring revenue

Both support contracts or agreements, recurring services, billing schedules, account history, and the recurring-revenue model central to managed services.

Sales-to-service handoff

Both can move approved quotes into operational work, preserving customer, product, pricing, and service information for fulfillment.

Inventory + assets

Both provide product, purchasing, inventory, and customer-asset concepts, although depth and implementation approach differ.

Integrations + reporting

Both connect to accounting, RMM, productivity, and other MSP systems and provide dashboards, reports, APIs, and workflow automation.

Bottom line: this is not a choice between “PSA” and “not a PSA.” It is a choice between two PSA operating models, levels of maturity, and implementation paths.

8 · Where New Rev.io differs

New Rev.io favors a modern, unified operating model; ConnectWise favors breadth, maturity, and deep configurability.

New Rev.io advantage: unified execution

  • Ticket-centered model connects service, projects, time, parts, assets, billing, and profitability
  • Standard users receive the technician-focused mobile experience
  • Offline mobile caching supports assigned work and recent history
  • Native payments and revenue workflows reduce payment-tool dependency
  • Modern interface creates an opportunity to simplify old processes rather than recreate them

ConnectWise advantage: mature MSP depth

  • Long-established MSP ecosystem with broad partner and consultant familiarity
  • Deep procurement, inventory, workflow-rule, reporting, and configuration history
  • Extensive feature coverage accumulated across decades of PSA development
  • Net Tech already has 19 years of data, integrations, habits, and institutional knowledge
  • Lower short-term change burden if the current environment is retained

New Rev.io watch items

  • Individual tasks inside project tickets are not independently assignable today
  • Complex skills-based scheduling and cascading dependencies require workflow proof
  • Portal role granularity and knowledge-base capabilities are still expanding
  • Inventory and hardware-rental fit needs a dedicated validation session

ConnectWise watch items for Net Tech

  • On-prem environment has become a “data lake” surrounded by bolt-ons
  • Moovila failed to resolve project-management needs
  • Inventory remains outside the PSA in SharePoint lists
  • Mobile adoption is limited and project changes require heavy manual rescheduling
  • Continuing may preserve complexity instead of solving it

9 · Net Tech decision framework

The right answer depends on whether Net Tech values ecosystem depth or a cleaner operating model more—and whether Rev.io can prove the complex workflows.

Choose New Rev.io if…

Net Tech wants to retire bolt-ons, modernize field work, connect service and revenue, simplify user experience, and redesign processes around a unified platform.

Stay with ConnectWise if…

Preserving every mature edge case, existing integration, customization, and historical workflow outweighs the operational friction of the current environment.

Do not decide until Rev.io proves…

A realistic 70–100+ task project, multi-technician scheduling, schedule-change handling, hardware rental and inventory, data migration, and feature-gap treatment.

Migration question

Can Net Tech move the data and processes that still create value without recreating nineteen years of technical debt in a new platform?

Reference question

What did comparable 20+ user ConnectWise customers gain, what surprised them, and which capabilities required a new way of working?

Commercial question

Does the total cost of New Rev.io—including implementation and change management—beat the ongoing cost of ConnectWise plus its bolt-ons and manual work?
