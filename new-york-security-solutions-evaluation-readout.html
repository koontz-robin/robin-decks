2. Business issue

NYSS needs one operating system for project-heavy security work, recurring services, and QBO revenue flow.

The business issue

NYSS sells and supports cloud security, access control, video surveillance, and multi-site environments. The work does not stop at installation — it becomes projects, service tickets, subscriptions, renewals, invoices, payments, and accounting handoffs.

Why now

As NYSS grows multi-site and cloud-based customer relationships, manual handoffs between sales, operations, billing, and accounting create more risk: missed recurring charges, delayed invoicing, unclear service ownership, and weaker project margin visibility.

Why Rev.io fits

Rev.io PSA gives NYSS one workflow for customer records, quoting, project/ticket execution, mobile field work, agreements, invoicing, payments, reporting, and QBO integration — the connective tissue behind the security lifecycle.

3. Challenges / DBMs uncovered in the sales process

The DBMs center on replacing fragmented operations with a cleaner quote-to-cash and service-management motion.

Project-to-service handoff

Installation and migration work needs to convert cleanly into ongoing support, warranty, asset history, recurring billing, and customer visibility without rekeying the same information in multiple places.

Multi-site customer complexity

NYSS needs customer, location, device, access, service-history, and billing visibility by property, campus, retail site, or business unit — not a flat account record that hides operational context.

Recurring revenue discipline

Cloud security platforms, monitoring, service plans, warranties, and renewals need clean agreement rules, activation controls, billing schedules, and reporting so recurring revenue does not rely on memory and spreadsheets.

Inventory, purchasing, and equipment

Security projects involve cameras, readers, controllers, networking gear, licenses, vendors, POs, receiving, replacements, and phased installs. Disconnected systems make margin and change-order control harder.

QBO billing confidence

The billing discussion surfaced a key decision point: Rev.io must make the QBO handoff clear enough that NYSS trusts invoices, payments, customers, products, and accounting sync scope before changing systems.

Executive visibility

Leadership needs a system that shows project status, open service work, recurring revenue, invoice status, customer/site health, and operational bottlenecks without custom reporting heroics.

7. Scope of the QuickBooks Online integration

QBO remains the accounting system; Rev.io becomes the operational billing source that sends clean financial data downstream.

Customer + invoice flow

  • Customer/account data and invoice details are created and managed operationally in Rev.io
  • Approved invoices sync to QBO for accounting visibility
  • Invoice status and accounting handoff should be validated in implementation

Payments + revenue

  • Rev.io can support payment capture and payment-account workflow
  • Payments can be reflected against invoices and sent through the QBO accounting process
  • Recurring charges should originate from Rev.io agreements / bill profiles

Products + GL mapping

  • Products, services, taxes, and revenue categories need mapped to QBO items/accounts
  • NYSS should confirm chart-of-accounts, item, class/location, and tax requirements
  • Exception handling should be defined before go-live

Implementation decisions

Confirm whether QBO is the single accounting endpoint, which customer/product/payment fields sync, how historical invoices are handled, who owns accounting exceptions, and whether class/location tracking is required for project or branch reporting.

What not to assume

Rev.io should not be positioned as replacing QBO. The cleaner posture: Rev.io manages quote-to-cash operations and revenue workflows; QBO remains the financial/accounting ledger.
