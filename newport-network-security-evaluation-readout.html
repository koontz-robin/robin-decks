Executive summary

Re-engagement earned. Readiness still needs proof.

The refreshed platform addressed several prior pain points in the demo—especially quote-level markup, dispatch visibility, parent/child flexibility, QuickBooks Desktop integration, and a more deliberate approach to product change. Newport’s decision should now turn on an operational proof of the remaining high-impact workflows.

Why revisit now

Newport stepped back previously because platform changes moved faster than teams could absorb and important workflows were incomplete. Rev.io described a near-term focus on performance and optimization, with fewer disruptive interface changes.

What improved

The session showed faster quote markup, combined one-time and recurring items, reusable macros, dispatch and GPS-assisted time prompts, CRM/account hierarchy improvements, ADI pricing availability, and a direct QuickBooks Desktop integration.

Current-state friction

The problem is confidence at the handoffs.

The prior experience created change fatigue. The evaluation now has to prove that quoting, service delivery, billing, and reporting work as one dependable operating motion—not just as separate screens.

Quoting and catalog control

  • Prior template creation and pricing adjustments felt clunky.
  • Cabling is often quoted and tracked by footage; fractional spool quantities remain an open gap.
  • Newport may need both native quoting and Portal for takeoffs/field quoting.

Complex customer structures

  • Work can occur at multiple school or end-customer sites while an electrical contractor is billed.
  • The same site may also be billed directly.
  • Tax treatment can differ by service location and billing relationship.

Operational adoption

  • Rapid product changes previously disrupted learned processes.
  • Field users need a practical mobile flow, not added administration.
  • Reports for tax and payroll must be usable by the people who own them.
Target outcomes

Define success in operating terms.

These outcomes translate the conversation into measurable evaluation objectives. Baselines and targets should be confirmed by Newport before a financial case is approved.

Faster, cleaner quote creation

Build common security, telecom, cabling, and service scopes with consistent pricing, margins, reusable bundles, and an acceptable customer-facing proposal.

Reliable service-to-cash flow

Move an approved scope into scheduled work, mobile execution, accurate time/material capture, invoice review, payment, and accounting without re-keying.

Control without surveillance

Use timer-based location context and technician prompts to improve ETA communication and time accuracy while limiting GPS visibility to active travel/work.

Correct multi-entity billing

Support contractor/end-customer relationships, multiple service addresses, simultaneous billing paths, and the correct tax result for each scenario.

Decision-ready reporting

Produce sales/use-tax, payroll, service, project, profitability, and operational reports that Newport can reconcile to known examples.

Manageable change

Give administrators a stable workflow, clear release communication, role-based permissions, and a realistic training/adoption plan.

Requirements & validation gates

Separate what was shown from what still needs proof.

“Shown” means demonstrated in the September 22 session—not independently accepted by Newport. “Open” means a follow-up demonstration or configuration proof is required. “Gap” identifies a known limitation or unresolved design.
Quote pricing and reusable bundles
Shown

Quote/item markup, identifiers, one-time + recurring services, macros, and PandaDoc presentation were demonstrated. Gate: Newport user rebuilds two representative quotes.

Fractional/spool material handling
Known gap

Fractional quantity was not available in the demonstrated workflow. Validate a footage-based catalog workaround, inventory impact, and roadmap disposition.

Portal and product-cost workflow
Open

Portal and ADI capabilities were discussed. Gate: prove the selected path from takeoff/catalog through quote, approval, fulfillment, and margin reporting.

Parent/child, multi-site, and tax rules
Critical

Hierarchy flexibility and multiple addresses were discussed; per-child tax rules require verification. Gate: invoice three Newport scenarios and reconcile tax/bill-to results.

Dispatch, calendar, and timer-based GPS
Shown

Drag/drop dispatch, calendar sync, active-timer location, and proximity prompts were demonstrated. Gate: field test with representative technician roles and consent policy.

Projects, tickets, workflows, mobile
Open

These were deferred for follow-up. Gate: execute one project job and one break/fix ticket from intake to invoice, including mobile time/material capture.

Reporting, payroll, sales/use tax
Open

Outstanding demo topics. Gate: Newport owners receive, filter, export, and reconcile required reports to source examples.

QuickBooks Desktop integration
Verify

A direct integration was stated as live. Gate: document supported objects, direction/frequency, error handling, duplicate prevention, and reconciliation ownership.

Revy permissions and confirmation controls
Shown

Permission-aware access and human confirmation before actions were described. Gate: test allowed/denied actions using Newport roles; confirm data/security details in writing.

Tailored proof plan

One working session. Six Newport scenarios.

Use a controlled sample—not production data—to test the full operating chain. Capture pass/fail, owner, evidence, workaround, and business impact for every gate.

Quote two real patterns

Build a standard security bundle and a footage-heavy cabling job. Apply markup, tax, alternates, recurring service, and a customer-ready proposal.

Prove Portal handoff

If Portal remains in scope, demonstrate one takeoff/field quote moving into Rev.io with items, pricing, customer, documents, and margin intact.

Run a project + service call

Convert approved work, assign resources, schedule against calendars, capture field notes/time/materials, change status, and prepare billing.

Test billing complexity

Model electrical-contractor billing, direct school-district billing, and simultaneous relationships across multiple taxable service locations.

Close the books

Create invoices/payments, sync a controlled batch to QuickBooks Desktop, force one correction/error, then reconcile totals and ownership.

Validate controls

Test role permissions, Revy confirmations, audit history, mobile/GPS boundaries, required fields, report scheduling, and administrative change control.
