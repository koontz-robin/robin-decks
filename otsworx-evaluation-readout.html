OTSworx
01 · Executive readout

The opportunity is meaningful. The decision should follow proof, not promises.

Why change

OTSworx supports managed IT and complex commercial/industrial construction. Today, information can be rebuilt across estimating, proposals, projects, tickets, purchasing, field work, and accounting.

Where Rev.io can help

Connect service, projects, inventory, procurement, billing, customer context, and mobile work in one operating environment—with QuickBooks and key ecosystem integrations validated.

What must be proven

Customer and project file organization, the real construction handoff, ticket-email handling, migration depth, and integration scope are the gates to a confident decision.

The strongest outcome is not simply replacing ConnectWise. It is giving a small, capable team a system that scales without rebuilding the same information at every handoff.
OTSworx
02 · What we heard

Complex projects and service work need one reliable operational backbone.

Files must follow the work

Large projects need usable organization for pre-sale, customer, project, and ticket documents—not one undifferentiated customer-level bucket.

Potential go / no-go

Construction data is rebuilt

Takeoffs, assemblies, bills of materials, phases, labor, deposits, milestones, and customer-facing detail need to flow without recreating the job.

Proposals must flex

Customer-specific forms and summary requirements can force hours of rework. OTSworx needs section totals, optional packages, and control of visible detail.

Ticket context gets disconnected

New emails can land outside the right ticket, requiring staff to find, associate, forward, or link conversations manually.

Purchasing needs confidence

Changing distributor prices, similar parts, product images, reservations, serial numbers, and job cost all need to stay accurate from quote through invoice.

Migration must earn trust

A difficult prior onboarding raises the bar. Historical tickets, files, assets, agreements, mappings, billing, training, and rollback all need explicit acceptance criteria.

05 · Value hypothesis

Measure the work before turning it into an ROI claim.

Ticket administration

Reported baseline: 1–5 hours per day finding and associating ticket communications. Confirm whether this is a team total, frequency, affected roles, and the portion eliminated by an accepted workflow.

Construction rework

Examples included roughly 100 hours of duplicated project effort and eight hours reworking one proposal. Confirm frequency and avoidable share on representative jobs.

Software and leakage

Compare like-for-like incumbent and future costs, including retained RMM. Separately sample missed billable labor/parts, collection, and gross profit recovered.

A credible business case will separate cash savings, capacity value, and revenue protection—and count each benefit only once.

No customer-validated hard ROI is presented here. The baseline examples are inputs for joint validation, not promised savings.

Resolve the decisive questions in the right order.

2. Real construction proof

3. Mirna’s ticket cases

Reproduce new-email and disconnected-conversation scenarios; show what is automatic, manual, linked, forwarded, and not mergeable.

4. Integration evidence

Provide written scope or a working demonstration for every must-have integration and supplier—without treating investigation as availability.

5. Migration plan

Deliver a scoped data inventory, sample migration, acceptance testing, reconciliation, training, cutover, support, and rollback plan.

6. Commercial decision

Confirm the written user mix, subscription, onboarding, migration, integrations, overlap, cancellation, approvers, and decision meeting.
