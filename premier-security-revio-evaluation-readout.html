2. Current-state friction

mHelpDesk forces the team to compensate with duplicate work, improvised identifiers, manual price checks, and unreliable field data.

Estimates create ghost customers

A full customer record is required before an estimate can be drafted. At roughly 50 estimates per month and a 50% non-customer rate, the database fills with records that should have remained leads.

Proposal work is duplicated

Proposals are created in an external AI tool, then manually rebuilt in mHelpDesk. The same work is entered twice before delivery even begins.

Purchase orders lack identity

Job numbers are repurposed as PO numbers, creating confusion when one job requires multiple orders from multiple distributors.

Pricing goes stale

Estimators, parts coordinators, and billing staff repeatedly verify prices online. Volatile categories such as hard drives drive 15-day quote validity and margin risk.

Offline work is unreliable

Basements, IT rooms, parking structures, coastal sites, and remote locations interrupt connectivity. Notes and timers fail to sync, leaving office staff without a dependable job record.

Schedules do not refresh

Technicians may need to log out and back in to see calendar changes. Stale schedules have sent people to the wrong job site—a very expensive way to discover an app refresh problem.

3. Target operating model

One connected workflow from lead and proposal through procurement, installation, service, invoicing, and recurring revenue.

01

Qualify

Create lightweight leads without polluting the customer database; score and advance real opportunities.

02

Use live ADI pricing, controlled item visibility, branded templates, options, and built-in e-signature.

03

Generate tickets and recurring services from approved work; schedule and notify the right technicians.

04

Create unique POs, reserve parts, work offline, capture time, notes, photos, and signatures.

05

Review service invoices, automate recurring billing, process payments, and sync financial records to QBO.

5. Decision-critical proof

The platform fit is strong; four items should be closed before signature so implementation begins with facts instead of folklore.

1. Technician price visibility

Confirm that mobile permissions can hide cost and pricing from field technicians while preserving the parts, labor, and task information they need to complete work.

2. Schedule-change delivery

Test email, SMS, and in-app notifications for reassigned or rescheduled work, including what happens while a device is offline and when it reconnects.

3. Vendor-pricing coverage

ADI covers approximately 80% of purchases. Document the operating process for Wesco, DMP, Bosch, DSX, Brivo, and Eagle Eye until additional integrations are available.

4. Migration and billing cutover

Define authoritative data sources across mHelpDesk and QuickBooks, then validate recurring schedules, customer balances, payment data, open jobs, inventory, and the January 1 cutover sequence.

Known boundary: workflow notifications can react to stage changes, but time-based opportunity follow-ups such as “three days after send” are not currently available in the demonstrated workflow engine.

6. Business impact

The clearest value comes from eliminating duplicate work, protecting margin, and giving office and field teams one reliable operating record.

Recover estimating capacity

Lead-first CRM and native quoting remove unnecessary customer records and the manual rebuild between an external proposal tool and mHelpDesk.

Protect gross margin

Live ADI pricing reduces repeated web lookups and the risk that a quote is approved against outdated product cost.

Reduce field-service failure

Offline work, automatic synchronization, and explicit schedule-change alerts reduce missing notes, incomplete timers, and wrong-site dispatches.

Scale recurring revenue cleanly

Automated billing and payment workflows can handle Premier’s quarterly and annual base today while supporting a move toward monthly billing where appropriate.

ROI discipline: The final case should use Premier’s actual proposal-reentry time, price-check effort, billing labor, missed technician time, wrong-site dispatches, and current software costs. No imaginary savings wearing a utility belt.
