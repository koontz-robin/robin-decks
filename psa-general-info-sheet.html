What PSA does

The operating layer for teams delivering recurring service and project work.

A PSA helps a service provider manage the lifecycle from customer request to delivered work to invoice. Rev.io PSA is designed to reduce handoffs between ticketing, dispatch, projects, contracts, assets, inventory, billing, payments, and reporting.

Service desk and ticketing

  • Create, route, prioritize, and track tickets
  • Manage SLAs, statuses, escalations, and service workflows
  • Capture technician work, notes, time, and follow-up

Dispatch and scheduling

  • Assign work to the right technician or team
  • Coordinate calendars, field work, and onsite activity
  • Support technician productivity and service visibility

Mobile technician workflow

  • Access tickets, schedules, customer context, and site history
  • Add notes, photos, files, and time from the field
  • Support faster, cleaner job updates while technicians are onsite

Projects and resource management

  • Plan projects, milestones, tasks, resources, and budgets
  • Track budgeted vs. actual time and project performance
  • Connect projects and service work to billing visibility

Quoting, approvals, and eSignature

  • Build quotes and proposals tied to customers and opportunities
  • Use eSignature to move from approval to execution faster
  • Reduce rekeying between sales, projects, and billing

Inventory and asset tracking

  • Track inventory, locations, customer assets, and job readiness
  • Connect assets and services to billing and support coverage
  • Reduce missed revenue from disconnected inventory workflows
The practical goal: fewer systems to reconcile, fewer missed billable items, better customer visibility, and a cleaner path from work performed to revenue captured.
Why Rev.io is different

Rev.io connects the PSA workflow to revenue operations.

Traditional PSA tools often focus on tickets and projects, then rely on separate tools or manual exports to handle billing, payment, tax, and customer-facing financial workflows. Rev.io is built for service providers that need operations and billing to stay connected.

Ticket-to-billing connection

Time, ticket activity, projects, inventory, recurring services, and billable work can flow into the billing process so revenue is less likely to leak between systems.

Customer portal and payments

Rev.io can support the customer experience beyond service delivery, including billing visibility, customer portal workflows, payment processing, and receivables operations.

Reporting and Revii AI

Dashboards and Revii-supported workflows help teams see ticket load, project status, revenue, assets, technician work, customer trends, and operational performance.

Common prospect use cases

Where Rev.io PSA usually creates the strongest fit.

Replacing legacy PSA tools

Teams evaluating ConnectWise, Autotask, HaloPSA, Syncro, Tigerpaw, or spreadsheets often want a more modern workflow across ticketing, projects, dispatch, assets, reporting, and billing.

Unifying PSA and billing

MSPs, telecom providers, UCaaS providers, and integrators often lose time when service delivery, recurring contracts, inventory, tax, and payments live in disconnected systems.

Improving technician and operations visibility

Dispatch, mobile notes, time capture, asset status, project budgets, SLA activity, and customer work history help teams run cleaner daily operations.

Scaling with fewer manual handoffs

Workflow automation, integrations, Revii AI, dashboards, and better system alignment help growing teams reduce duplicated entry and protect revenue capture.

Questions to ask next

A practical discovery checklist for fit.

Current workflow

  • What PSA, RMM, CRM, accounting, quoting, and billing tools are in place today?
  • Which manual handoffs create the most rework or missed revenue?
  • What is the renewal timeline for current tools?

Must-have integrations

  • Which integrations are required on day one?
  • Which systems can be phased in after go-live?
  • Is API/webhook work needed for any custom systems?

Buying criteria

  • What workflows must be proven in a demo?
  • Who owns operations, finance, field team adoption, and executive approval?
  • What would make switching worth the effort?
