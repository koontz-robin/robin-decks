Company profile
A Growing, Multi-Service MSP with Complex Delivery

Enitech is a Raleigh-based managed IT provider serving SMBs across North Carolina and nationwide. Its work spans managed IT, cybersecurity, cloud, backup, vCIO, field services, structured cabling, telecom consulting and AV—mixing recurring support with onsite and project delivery.

  • 21 employees and $1.4M annual revenue in Salesforce; 14 team members publicly listed.
  • Serves security- and uptime-sensitive verticals including healthcare, dental, retail and nonprofits.
  • Named to the 2024 and 2025 MSP 501; founder Antwine Jackson is active in the MSP community.
Value planner · business issue
Disconnected Tools Were Limiting Scale
  • Autotask/Kaseya lacked the mobile experience field technicians needed.
  • Weak project management forced Enitech into a separate Monday.com workflow.
  • Agreement billing could not support the service models Enitech sells.
  • Benji Pays + QuickBooks sat outside the PSA, fragmenting payments and finance workflows.
  • A possible move from Datto RMM to NinjaRMM created further integration uncertainty.
Value planner · solution and value
One Back Office, Built Around How Enitech Operates

Stated value: “All back office functionality on one platform.”

Why Rev.io won
The Relationship Reduced Product and Migration Risk
  • Power: President Antwine Jackson was the final decision maker; Head of Finance Jessica Jackson was an active influencer.
  • Competitive context: Halo was evaluated in parallel and the Autotask contract was nearing expiration.
  • Proof: Team demos covered ticketing, billing, projects, mobile and technical workflows; Rev.io offered API guidance, sample-data migration support and direct roadmap access.
  • Differentiator: Enitech wanted a partner that would listen—community leadership and executive access made that credible.
Win story: Rev.io converted a competitor-displacement event into a 29-day close by connecting Enitech’s fragmented operating model to a clear consolidation outcome, then surrounding feature risk with technical proof, migration planning, roadmap transparency and a trusted community relationship.

A Field-Service Integrator Built for Complex, Distributed Work

R-Options is a Rochester-based low-voltage, security and AV integrator serving local institutions and national, multi-site customers. It delivers structured cabling, access control, surveillance, commercial AV, design, project management and 24/7 banking service.

  • $4.46M revenue and 20 employees in Salesforce; website claims 30+ Upstate field technicians.
  • Projects range from one-cable service calls to nationwide rollouts and full facility buildouts.
  • A partner network of 10,000+ technicians creates significant dispatch, purchasing and quality-control complexity.
Value planner · business issue
Manual Operations Created Risk Across the Entire Job Lifecycle
  • Paper-heavy workflows constrained technicians, office staff and leadership.
  • QuickBooks carried accounting, reporting and control processes that could not be disrupted.
  • Purchasing and inventory struggled when actual materials differed from quoted quantities.
  • Ticketing, dispatch, projects, payroll data, tax and compliance documents lacked one operating system.

A Converged Communications and Managed IT Provider

  • $7.97M annual revenue and 11 employees in Salesforce.

Sales Enablement

From Reactive Coverage to Direct Manager Ownership

Q4 2026 Board Meeting
Previous operating model
Shared, Request-Driven Coverage
Leadership structure31 director + 2 managers
AE population8Shared across leadership
Team ownershipDiffuseNo dedicated manager team
  • One director and two managers collectively covered all eight AEs.
  • Leadership joined any sales meeting when requested.
  • Coverage was reactive and spread across the full team.
  • Coaching ownership and rep accountability could shift between leaders.
  • Manager capacity was consumed by broad meeting support rather than a consistent team cadence.
Current operating model
Two Teams with Dedicated Ownership
Leadership structure2Director role eliminated
AE teams2One manager owns each
AE coverage~4:1Approx. AEs per manager
  • The director position was eliminated and the AEs were split into two teams.
  • Each manager has direct ownership of one team and its results.
  • Dedicated coverage blocks create predictable access to deal and meeting support.
  • Coaching is specialized to each rep’s skill gaps, pipeline, and active opportunities.
  • Daily stand-ups inspect meetings, preparation, desired outcomes, and next steps.
Board takeaway: The redesign reduced a management layer while increasing clarity of ownership. Managers now coach a defined team and provide proactive, structured coverage instead of sharing reactive meeting support across all eight AEs.
