05 · Highest-value qualifying questions

Close these inputs before presenting a business case.

Capacity baseline: Is 8–9 hours/day combined across Ed and Lisa, and what exact activities/time slices make it up?
Economic conversion: What are loaded hourly costs, and could released capacity avoid or delay the planned hire?
Demand: How many new tickets, phone calls, recurring inspections and contractor invoices occur monthly?
Failure cost: How often does urgent work age unnoticed, and what does delay cost in emergency premiums, downtime or branch risk?
Backlog truth: What do “9,900 overdue” and “45,000–55,000 unclear” each represent? How many records truly require migration?
Capital leverage: What was last year's repair spend by category, and which repeat repairs should have triggered replacement sooner?
Commercial hurdle: What is Rev.io's one-time and annual cost for the confirmed office/field user mix?
Power & process: Who owns budget, signs, approves security/procurement and decides whether year-end cutover risk is acceptable?
Success criteria: At 30/60/90 days, what reductions in chasing time, aged urgent tickets and unresolved statuses define success?
Migration: Which Tiger Paw/Odoo entities, attachments, drawings and history are mandatory, and who owns cleansing?
Questions prioritize the missing variables that determine ROI, commercial viability, technical fit and year-end feasibility.
06 · Execute plan

Move from a good demo to a controlled decision.

STEP 1

Prove the workflow

Run one overhead-door request from intake through escalation, contractor handoff, completion evidence, AP handoff and closure.

STEP 2

Baseline the value

Time-sample the current week; validate ticket/call/inspection volumes, delay events, rework and loaded labor cost.

STEP 3

Scope cost & migration

Confirm office/field licenses, product cost, Tiger Paw/Odoo data, attachment permissions, cleansing and cutover resources.

STEP 4

Approve against gates

Document fit/gaps, net ROI/payback, implementation risk, owner commitments and a dated go/no-go decision.

Decision gates

  • Critical facilities workflows demonstrated without hidden manual work
  • ROI inputs replaced with R.E. Michel baselines
  • Annual benefit clears total annualized cost at an agreed hurdle
  • Migration plan fits the year-end constraint—or an accepted bridge exists

Current assessment

  • Strategic value: high—the coordination burden is explicit
  • Workflow fit: plausible, not yet proven
  • ROI: directionally positive, not decision-grade
  • Execution risk: elevated due to migration timing and data quality
