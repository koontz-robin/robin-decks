02 · Why change now?

Alarm 7 is not just inconvenient. It is a continuity risk.

Legacy core

Unsupported Alarm 7 requires Windows 11 and Outlook workarounds; quoting and much inventory work remain in Word/Excel.

→

Operational drag

Install updates may arrive a day late, paper can be lost, truck stock is not consistently tracked and reporting depends on custom SQL/PowerShell.

→

2027 target state

One controlled workflow for quote, ticket, dispatch, mobile work, office review, recurring billing, payment and reporting.

Payments

Check-only payment is a frequent customer complaint; credit card and ACH are desired.

Adoption

The system must be simple enough for technicians who may skip optional steps; standard procedures matter more than AI.

Timing

Target discussed as beginning of 2027, sequenced around current server and backup work—not a committed go-live date.

Sources: Oct. 15 discovery and Oct. 16 demo notes.
03 · ROI architecture

Four value pools—kept separate to prevent double counting.

Discovery established the problems, but not enough operating baselines for a defensible savings claim. Each formula below shows exactly what must be measured.

1 · Quote administration

Baseline
Sourced A quote takes roughly 30–60 minutes.
Need
Data needed Quotes/month and time saved using approved templates.
Formula
quotes/mo × hours saved/quote × 12
Boundary
Counts sales/admin preparation only—not field or billing time.

2 · Field-to-office handoff

Baseline
Sourced Install entries can lag by one day; invoice review is a few minutes per ticket when notes are legible.
Need
Data needed Monthly field jobs and avoidable back-office minutes/job.
Formula
field jobs/mo × admin hours saved/job × 12
Boundary
Counts office re-entry/reconciliation only—not technician work.

3 · Reporting effort

Baseline
Sourced Custom SQL/PowerShell supports alarm-status, bad-debt and other exception reporting.
Need
Data needed Current monthly report creation/maintenance hours.
Formula
report hours avoided/mo × 12
Boundary
Excludes quote and ticket administration already counted above.

4 · Payment handling

Baseline
Sourced Customer payments are check-only today.
Need
Data needed Checks/month, handling time, DSO and payment-processing economics.
Formula
checks shifted × handling hours/check × 12
Boundary
Models labor only; no speculative revenue or cash-flow benefit is counted.
All value logic is a measurement framework, not a claimed outcome. No recurring-revenue uplift is assumed.

05 · Demonstrated fit & validation gates

The workflow fit is strong. The integration and adoption details still need proof.

Demonstrated

  • Quote templates, margin visibility and eSign
  • Ticket/dispatch workflow and mobile app
  • Travel/work timers, notes, photos and signatures
  • Office review before invoicing
  • Projects, job costing and recurring services
  • Read-only SQL lab and dashboards

Priority outcomes

  • Current field-to-office visibility
  • Consistent time, material and asset capture
  • Online card/ACH payment options
  • Monitoring-billing control
  • Simpler reporting without losing SQL access
  • Preserve Seaside’s local customer experience

Validate before commitment

  • Alarm 7 export and migration scope
  • QuickBooks Desktop workflow
  • Central Arm requirements/options
  • Phone GPS evidence vs. Verizon needs
  • Portal branding availability
  • Final standard/field license mix

Adoption controls

  • Required ticket tasks and completion gates
  • Install/service SOP alignment
  • Role-based permissions; AI optional
  • Pilot with representative field users
  • Offline workflow testing
  • Training aligned to IT capacity
Do not overpromise: the calls did not confirm that phone GPS fully replaces vehicle tracking or that Central Arm integration is available. Both remain validation items.

07 · Decision plan

Move from a good demo to an evidence-backed implementation decision.

1

Baseline

Seaside supplies quote/job/check volumes, reporting effort, loaded rates, payment metrics and final user split.

2

Validate

Joint technical session covers Alarm 7 extracts, QuickBooks Desktop, Central Arm, GPS evidence and required custom reports.

3

Pilot the behavior

Test technician entry, offline use, mandatory steps, install/service handoff and office invoice approval with real scenarios.

4

Decide & phase

Review final pricing, quantified ROI, migration controls and a 60–90 day onboarding plan aligned with IT availability.

Decision criteria

  • Critical integrations have documented answers
  • Field workflow is simple enough to enforce
  • Seaside baselines replace calculator assumptions
  • First-year value clears the validated cost
  • Migration and cutover controls are accepted

Highest-value unanswered questions

  • How many quotes and field jobs occur each month?
  • How much office time is spent re-entering or chasing job details?
  • How many checks are handled, and what is current DSO?
  • How many hours maintain SQL/PowerShell reports?
  • What are final license, implementation and processing costs?
Onboarding timing discussed on calls remains subject to scope. Target timing is a planning horizon, not a commitment.

Can Rev.io replace an unsupported operational core with a field-to-office workflow Seaside’s team will actually use—and produce measurable value at a validated cost?
