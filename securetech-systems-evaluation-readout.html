1. Business profile

Security systems built to last—designed once, done right.

Who SecureTech serves

SecureTech is an MBE-certified integrator designing commercial security cameras, access control, intercom, low-voltage, networking, and integrated building technology across Long Island, New York City, upstate New York, and beyond.

How the business operates

The team supports multi-location commercial customers plus select high-end residential work. Four office users manage quoting and operations while field technicians currently work outside the core software.

What cannot go backward

Any replacement must equal or beat iPoint’s parent-child accounts, proposal revisions, quote templates, live margin visibility, recurring invoices, QuickBooks flow, and searchable item history.

2. Business issue

The front office works—but growth is exposing the seams between quoting, projects, technicians, inventory, service, and finance.

Margin is exposed

Vendor costs are not reliably refreshing into iPoint. Small price changes multiplied across large equipment quantities create real margin leakage unless the team manually verifies every quote.

Project work lacks structure

iPoint’s project and work-order functions are considered too complicated, so Google Calendar and Google Chat have become the practical job file, schedule, photo log, and field communication layer.

Labor is disconnected

Gusto captures payroll hours but not time by project. SecureTech cannot compare quoted labor to actual technician effort or see job profitability developing in real time.

Service is informal

Support emails and non-billable issues are not consistently ticketed, measured, or tied to customer and asset history—limiting service-desk accountability as the company grows.

Inventory visibility is thin

Items sold can be found, but serial numbers, MAC addresses, warranties, RMAs, truck stock, reservations, and installed-asset service history are not managed as one lifecycle.

Recurring revenue needs control

Monitoring, service contracts, and cloud services span monthly, quarterly, and annual billing, but customers are not consistently governed by formal agreements and profitability controls.

3. Current system map

Today’s workflow is capable, but every major handoff crosses a system boundary.

iPoint

Core CRM, proposals, sales orders, invoices, recurring billing, item database, and early purchase-order use. Project/work-order features are largely unused because of complexity.

Portal

Product catalog and vendor pricing source. The legacy account may be contributing to sync failures; accurate real-time pricing is the top evaluation priority.

QuickBooks Online

Accounting and AR record. Invoices are manually pushed from iPoint, while payment status and receipts flow through the current payment process.

HubSpot

Prospecting, email marketing, lists, follow-ups, and email tracking. It is completely separate from iPoint today, forcing a manual transition when a prospect becomes quote-ready.

Google Calendar + Chat

Scheduling, walkthrough notes, photos, BOMs, job instructions, daily field updates, and site history. The approach is familiar but increasingly noisy and difficult to govern.

System Surveyor + Gusto + Metrodial

System Surveyor handles drawings/design; Gusto handles payroll clock-in; Metrodial is the central station. None currently forms a complete connected operating workflow with iPoint.

The value is not replacing screens—it is protecting margin and making every job, asset, hour, contract, and invoice measurable.
