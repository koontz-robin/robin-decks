Executive summary

The case for change is operational—not cosmetic.

Slice has consensus to replace Autotask, which currently sits beside QuickBooks Desktop, spreadsheets, and a separate pre-sales CRM. The central issue is broken handoffs: upgrades and installs can miss billing, technicians complete tickets after the fact, and simple quotes take about 30 minutes. Rev.io should be judged on whether one controlled workflow closes those gaps and produces trustworthy financial outputs.
1

Why change

Manual relay between service and billing creates leakage and re-keying. Project workflows have not worked for Slice, renewal reminders are weak, and field adoption is low.

2

Why now

The team has revisited replacement attempts, completed an end-to-end demo, and moved to product/contract validation. Contract review is the recorded next step for November 10.

QuickBooks Desktop sync, quote speed, upgrade-to-billing control, field time capture, project execution, migration, renewal reminders, and reporting must pass with Slice scenarios.

Who Slice is

WiFi operations with field and financial complexity.

Salesforce records 12 employees and a team supporting quoting, installs, service, recurring upgrades, and finance. Public website context is used only for this high-level description.

Current stack

Autotask for CRM, quoting, and service; QuickBooks Desktop for accounting; spreadsheets for estimates; and a separate pre-sales CRM.

Operating motion

WiFi equipment quotes, installs, service tickets, recurring bandwidth upgrades, project work, and customer contracts.

Commercial scope recorded

Problems & consequences

Value leaks at each handoff.

The discovery record supports the issues below. The discovery and tailored demo records support the issues below; the business case later in this readout uses conservative, labeled assumptions where the transcript lacks volume.

Slow quoting

A simple Autotask quote takes about 30 minutes versus about 5 minutes in John’s spreadsheet, creating avoidable admin time and tool switching.

Missed billing

Service upgrades can fail to reach billing a few times per quarter. Past installs reportedly went unbilled for a month before process cleanup.

Delayed field records

Technicians do not use Autotask consistently in the field and complete tickets later, weakening time accuracy and install profitability visibility.

Fragmented customer lifecycle

Leads, customers, estimates, work, billing, and accounting span separate systems and manual messages.

Weak project and renewal control

Autotask project management did not work for larger projects, and contract renewal reminders are described as inadequate.

Finance confidence

The CFO wants install time tracked and is considering QuickBooks Online, making accounting boundaries and migration sequencing material.
