Systems Northwest × Rev.ioOperational value case
01 · Why evaluate now

Growth has made the handoffs between systems more expensive—and service more difficult to coordinate.

Disconnected workflows

Sales, estimating, projects, purchasing, time, service, and accounting each rely on different tools, files, and manual handoffs.

A service division taking shape

Shared technicians are pulled between installation and service, creating backlog risk while Systems Northwest establishes dedicated service capacity.

Hard-to-find operating data

Installed assets, serial numbers, warranties, inventory status, and customer history can require searching through nested folders—or a trip to the jobsite.

Evaluation thesis: A shared operational record can reduce time spent moving and searching for information while making service, purchasing, and inventory status easier to see.
Why changeBased on discovery · November 17, 2026
02 · Current operating model

The business is managed across capable tools—but people carry the workflow between them.

Current environment

Portal.ioOffice 365DropboxExcel + WordQuickBooks EnterpriseTimerSortie

Portal supports most sales proposals; construction estimating, project tracking, service intake, purchasing, inventory, accounting, and timekeeping follow separate processes.

Where friction shows up

  • Project folders move from proposal to project to customer records.
  • Service calls can be missed before they reach the Excel log.
  • Purchasing status travels through email and Dropbox.
  • Inventory relies largely on spreadsheets and quarterly counts.
  • Historical asset and warranty research can require extensive searching.
Important distinction: QuickBooks entry itself was described as quick. The larger opportunity is reducing the surrounding search, re-entry, status chasing, and handoffs.
Current stateMultiple systems · manual handoffs
03 · Target future state

A single operational record from opportunity through service—while QuickBooks remains the accounting ledger.

Customer + project record

Keep customer history, job status, documents, labor, materials, and service activity connected instead of rebuilding context across folders.

Service intake + dispatch

Capture requests consistently, see technician capacity, prioritize work, and reduce dependence on a single spreadsheet or inbox.

Purchasing + inventory

Connect material requests, purchase orders, receipts, locations, project allocations, and installed assets to the work they support.

Serialized assets + warranty

Preserve equipment history by customer and site so teams can find model, serial, installation, and warranty information quickly.

Field execution

Give technicians one mobile workflow for assignments, time, notes, photos, parts, and completion details.

Connected billing handoff

Validate the required QuickBooks Enterprise workflow so completed work and billing data move with less manual coordination.

Future stateOne source of operational truth

06 · What the evaluation should prove

Before final ROI, validate the workflows and measure the baseline.

Workflow proof

Service operations

Demonstrate phone/email intake, triage, dispatch, technician availability, mobile execution, and escalation using Systems Northwest examples.

Workflow proof

Inventory + assets

Test staged project equipment, stock locations, serialization, warranty lookup, purchasing, receiving, and customer-site assets.

Integration proof

QuickBooks + Portal

Confirm QuickBooks Enterprise edition/version and required invoice, payment, tax, and GL flows; validate Portal data direction and handoff.

Integration proof

USA Central Station

Confirm whether the required monitoring workflow is supported before it becomes part of scope or commercial expectations.

Baseline

Time recovered

Measure search, scheduling, re-entry, purchasing follow-up, and field administration time; clarify whether daily and weekly scheduling estimates overlap.

Baseline

Cost + leakage

Validate software costs, transition effort, inventory adjustments, unbilled work, delayed billing, and the portion that is realistically recoverable.
